Passphrase Generator

Memorable because it paints a picture; strong because every highlighted word is drawn uniformly by your browser's cryptographic random number generator. The grammar around them is fixed and public, so it adds no secret bits and can be rebuilt from the words alone.

How long would it take to guess?

Websites and password managers store a hash of your password. If that hash is stolen, an attacker can guess offline as fast as their hardware allows, so the hashing algorithm sets the real cost of each guess. Average time to guess this passphrase:

For a password manager's master password, use Argon2id with plenty of memory and at least 5 words. Passwords stored inside the manager can be shorter.

How the bits are counted

Assume the attacker has this page: the word lists and sentence structure are public. Only the random word choices are secret, and each is drawn uniformly:

The whole sentence is one uniform draw over every possible sentence, so its strength is exactly log₂(number of sentences), the sum of the bits above.

Words are chosen to be easy to picture and spell: concrete, well known and common in the Brysbaert et al. (2014) concreteness ratings, in base form according to WordNet, and screened by hand for offensive words.

Re-rolling until you like a sentence costs very little. Generating a big batch and hand-picking a favorite is worse, because people's favorites are easier to predict.
